The studio smelled faintly of hairspray, burnt coffee, and overheated camera equipment.

Rihan hated all three.

The smell clung to the air like exhaustion itself thick, artificial, impossible to ignore. It mixed with the constant heat coming from the giant filming lights overhead, making the entire place feel stuffy despite the expensive air conditioning humming somewhere above.

He sat silently in front of the mirror while a makeup artist pressed cold foundation against his skin with enough force to qualify as personal revenge.

"Stop moving."

"I'm literally breathing."

"Exactly. Stop."

Rihan stared at his reflection with the expression of a man reconsidering every life decision that had brought him here.

First commercial shoot after getting signed to the agency.

And somehow he already wanted to fake his own death.

The makeup artist grabbed his chin and tilted his face slightly toward the light.

"You have good skin," she muttered.

"Thanks."

"But your expression looks like you're attending a funeral."

"That's because I'm here at seven in the morning."

"That's fair."

Around him, the studio moved with chaotic rhythm.

Stylists rushed past carrying clothing racks that looked expensive enough to require insurance.

Managers barked instructions into headsets while simultaneously drinking iced americanos like their lives depended on caffeine.

Assistants sprinted from one side of the set to the other carrying makeup kits, extension cords, steaming equipment, and paper cups full of coffee nobody ever seemed to finish.

Somewhere behind him, two staff members were arguing over lighting angles with the intensity of divorced parents discussing child custody.

Another person loudly yelled:

"WHO MOVED CAMERA THREE?"

A different voice immediately yelled back:

"IT WASN'T ME."

"THEN WHY ARE YOU SWEATING?"

Normal industry madness.

Rihan adjusted the sleeves of his cream-colored sweater and glanced down at his phone again.

Still nothing.

No messages.

No missed calls.

No unread notifications.

Perfect.

He slid the phone back into his pocket with relief.

People always thought becoming a model or actor meant loving attention.

Rihan personally considered attention a medically concerning experience.

Even being around this many strangers already drained half his remaining lifespan.

"Relax your shoulders," the makeup artist said.

"I am relaxed."

"You look like you're being interrogated."

"That's just my face."

She sighed dramatically before continuing her work.

Rihan looked toward the filming area instead.

The set itself was ridiculously bright.

Large white panels reflected artificial light everywhere, making the entire studio glow unnaturally clean. Several fake bathroom props had been arranged nearby for the toothpaste commercial they were shooting today.

A fake sink.

Fake marble counter.

Fake plants.

Probably fake happiness too.

He still didn't fully understand how he ended up here.

Three months ago, he was just another university student trying to survive assignments, cheap convenience store meals, and a sleep schedule held together by caffeine.

Then one accidental street casting later, suddenly agencies were calling him "marketable."

He still wasn't completely sure what that meant.

Probably tall.

Maybe emotionally unavailable.

Those seemed to work in this industry.

"Your co-actor is here!"

The announcement echoed casually through the studio.

But somehow the atmosphere changed instantly.

Conversations paused.

People straightened up.

One stylist nearly dropped an entire makeup palette.

A staff member hurriedly fixed their hair using their phone camera reflection.

Even the managers suddenly sounded more polite.

Rihan frowned slightly.

Who exactly were they acting this dramatic for?

Then he heard the voice.

"Why does every studio in Seoul smell like exhausted people?"

His head lifted automatically.

And every functioning thought in his brain immediately packed its bags and left.

Mira Han.

Actress.

YouTuber.

Singer occasionally.

Professional menace constantly.

Owner of twenty million followers and approximately seventy percent of the nation's male attention span.

She walked into the studio wearing an oversized black hoodie, loose gray sweatpants, sneakers, and sunglasses that somehow made her even more recognizable instead of less.

Her hair looked slightly messy, like she had tied it up too quickly before leaving home.

Not messy in a bad way.

Messy in the deeply unfair way attractive people somehow managed to make look intentional.

A few strands framed her face naturally while the rest fell over her shoulders in soft waves.

Two staff members rushed over nervously to greet her.

Mira immediately bowed politely.

"Sorry I'm late," she said.

"You're actually early," one manager replied quickly.

"Oh. That's concerning."

A few people laughed instantly.

Rihan looked away before he accidentally stared too long.

Nope.

Absolutely not.

He refused to become one of those guys who forgot how to behave around pretty women.

He had dignity.

Minimal dignity.

But still dignity.

Unfortunately, fate seemed personally invested in humiliating him today.

Because only seconds later, Mira looked around the room before her eyes landed directly on him.

And then she started walking toward him.

Rihan's survival instincts immediately activated.

Unfortunately, there was nowhere to escape.

"You're Rihan, right?"

Up close, she looked even worse for his mental stability.

The sunglasses were gone now.

Which honestly felt unfair.

Pictures online genuinely did not do her justice. There was something strangely alive about her in personexpressive eyes, easy posture, warmth that cameras couldn't fully capture.

He stood awkwardly from his chair.

"…Yeah."

"I'm Mira."

"I know."

Silence.

The exact moment the words left his mouth, Rihan felt his soul leave his body in embarrassment.

Fantastic.

Amazing.

Incredible first impression.

Why stop there?

Maybe he should walk directly into traffic afterward just to complete the experience.

For one horrifying second, he prepared for the awkwardness to permanently destroy his future career.

Then Mira blinked once.

And laughed.

Not fake celebrity laughter.

Not the practiced kind people used for interviews and variety shows.

Real laughter.

Bright and sudden.

The kind that escaped naturally and slightly crinkled the corners of her eyes.

"That's somehow embarrassing for me," she said.

Rihan rubbed the back of his neck awkwardly.

"No I meant you're famous."

"Ah." She nodded thoughtfully. "Good recovery. I've heard worse."

"Really?"

"One guy met me and accidentally said 'I love you' instead of hello."

Rihan stared.

"…Did you marry him after that?"

She snorted unexpectedly.

"See? You're already funnier than most actors here."

"I think the standards might just be low."

"They are."

The conversation settled strangely easily after that.

Which honestly confused him more than anything.

Usually talking to new people felt exhausting.

Like trying to perform correctly in a situation everyone else understood naturally except him.

But Mira spoke casually, like there was no pressure to impress her.

No weird celebrity distance.

No fake politeness.

Just… normal.

Which somehow made her feel even more dangerous.

A stylist approached Mira carefully.

"We'll start touch-ups in five minutes."

"Got it."

The stylist left after bowing slightly.

Mira looked back at Rihan again.

"You nervous?"

"A little."

"That's normal. Commercial shoots are weird."

"You say that like you've survived war."

"I've filmed sixteen ads pretending yogurt changed my life."

"…That does sound traumatic."

"Thank you for understanding."

Rihan laughed quietly before he could stop himself.

And that seemed to surprise both of them equally.

Mira tilted her head slightly.

"Oh, you actually smile."

"Rare condition."

"I thought your face was naturally emotionally devastated."

"That's also true."

She laughed again.

And annoyingly enough, hearing it felt nice.

Too nice.

One assistant suddenly rushed toward them holding a clipboard.

"Five-minute warning!"

The peaceful atmosphere instantly broke again as the studio returned to chaos.

Staff members moved quickly across the set adjusting lights and camera positions.

Someone rolled in additional equipment.

A manager shouted something about brand placement.

Rihan exhaled softly.

Work mode.

Right.

That existed.

Mira noticed his expression immediately.

"You'll be fine," she said casually.

"Hm?"

"Your face right now." She pointed at him lightly. "You look like you're preparing for execution."

"That obvious?"

"Painfully."

He looked toward the filming set again.

Truthfully, he wasn't scared of acting badly.

He was scared of being watched.

Hundreds of eyes.

Cameras.

Expectations.

The possibility of messing up in front of people who already knew exactly what they were doing.

Mira suddenly stepped beside him.

"Wanna know a secret?"

"What?"

"Everyone here is pretending they know what they're doing."

Rihan glanced at her.

She shrugged lightly.

"Some people are just better at pretending."

For some reason, that helped more than motivational speeches probably should've.

A staff member called for them again.

Mira started walking backward toward the makeup area before stopping briefly.

"Oh, and Rihan?"

"Yeah?"

"If you accidentally say something embarrassing again, commit confidently. It works better."

He stared at her for a second.

Then sighed.

"That advice feels experience-based."

"You have no idea."

She flashed him one last grin before disappearing toward the stylists.

Rihan stood there quietly for a moment afterward.

The studio was still loud.

Still chaotic.

Still smelled like burnt coffee and overheated equipment.

But somehow the atmosphere felt lighter now.

Warmer.

And weirdly enough…

That scared him more than Mira Han's fame ever could.
